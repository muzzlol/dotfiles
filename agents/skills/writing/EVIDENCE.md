# Evidence — writing skill

The backing for every rule in [SKILL.md](SKILL.md), graded by source strength (scale below), following the proompting skill's evidence discipline: label every claim by what supports it, and treat a citation as evidence only once checked. Claims were verified against sources in August 2026 (web research; existence and content of each source checked). Consult the matching entry before adding to, weakening, or deleting a rule, and check **Debunked** before admitting new advice.

Grades: **meta** (meta-analysis) · **replicated** (multiple independent studies or programs) · **single** (one study or one lab's program) · **preprint** (recent, not yet peer-reviewed) · **practitioner** (large-scale field practice or deployed guidance, no controlled study) · **derived** (inference from adjacent findings, untested directly).

## The frame

- **Readers satisfice; the exit decision happens at unit starts.** Pirolli & Card 1999, *Psychological Review* 106 (information foraging: readers maximize expected information gain and leave when it drops); Duggan & Payne 2009, *JEP: Applied* 15 (skimmers under time pressure retain important ideas, lose details) and 2011 CHI eyetracking (gaze concentrates on first halves of paragraphs and pages; satisficing model fits). — replicated.
- **Little of a page is read.** Nielsen 2008 "How Little Do Users Read?", regression over Weinreich et al. 2008 browser logs (25 users, 45k page views): ~20–28% of words; each +100 words buys ~4.4s of attention. — single; treat magnitudes as illustrative.
- **Screen reading is shallower than paper for expository text.** Delgado, Vargas, Ackerman & Salmerón 2018, *Educational Research Review* meta (54 studies, >170k participants): paper advantage g ≈ 0.21, expository only, larger under time pressure, growing over time. — meta.
- **Fragments backfire.** ASD-STE100 Issue 9 bans ellipsis (dropping subjects/articles/verbs) because it creates ambiguity — practitioner. Davison & Kantor 1982, *Reading Research Quarterly*: shortening sentences to satisfy readability formulas deletes the connectives readers need and can reduce comprehension. — replicated critique. Basis of "select, don't compress".
- **The curse of knowledge is real and empathy doesn't fix it.** Camerer, Loewenstein & Weber 1989, *Journal of Political Economy* (informed traders can't discount their knowledge; incentives halve the bias at best); Ryskin & Brown-Schmidt 2014, *PLoS ONE* (7 experiments: replicates at roughly half the folklore effect size, reliably nonzero); Pinker 2014, *The Sense of Style* ch. 3 (introspective "imagine the reader" is weak debiasing). Newton's 1990 tappers/listeners demo (2.5% vs predicted 50%) is an unpublished dissertation — illustration only; hang the claim on Camerer/Nickerson 1999. — replicated. Basis of the mechanical Check.
- **Rater preference is not reader value.** Singhal, Goyal, Xu & Durrett 2023 (arXiv 2310.03716): RLHF gains largely reduce to length. LMSYS style control, Aug 2024: length is the dominant style coefficient in human votes (0.249 vs ~0.02–0.03 for markdown features). Zhang et al. 2024 "From Lists to Emojis" (arXiv 2409.11704): humans, GPT-4 judges, and reward models all over-reward lists/bold/emoji. Sharma et al., ICLR 2024 (arXiv 2310.13548): raters non-negligibly prefer convincing sycophantic answers over correct ones. — replicated. Basis of "do not trust the feel".

## Order

- **Answer first.** DeAngelo & Yegiyan 2018/2019, *Journalism & Mass Communication Quarterly*: inverted-pyramid stories read in less time and recalled better than narrative order. Emde, Klimmt & Schluetz 2016, *Journalism Studies* (N=706 adolescents): narrative order helps low-prior-knowledge readers — the reversal case. BLUF is doctrine (US Army AR 25-50), not experiment. NN/g 2025 "How AI Is Changing Search Behaviors": reading stops at sufficiency. — single per side, directionally consistent for skilled task-oriented adults; contested for novices on complex events.
- **Front-load every unit.** Morkes & Nielsen 1997 / CHI 98: 79% of users scan; concise+scannable+objective rewrites improved a composite usability score (+124%; small N — illustrative). Pernice 2017/2019 (NN/g eyetracking, 47+ participants): layer-cake scanning (headings + first lines) is the most effective pattern short of full reading; the F-pattern appears when formatting gives no anchors and causes missed content. — practitioner, consistent with Duggan & Payne (replicated).
- **Size tracks the question.** "How Does Conversation Length Impact User's Satisfaction?" CHI 2024 EA: added length yields repetition, not helpfulness. YapBench (arXiv 2601.00624, 2026): order-of-magnitude excess over minimal-sufficient answers across 76 assistants, tied to preference training. NN/g 2023 "Accordion Editing": users repeatedly ask for shorter versions and fight scroll. — preprint + practitioner.
- **Summaries can beat full text.** Reder & Anderson 1980, *JVLVB* 19 (and 1982 follow-up): studying summaries matched or beat full chapters for main-point retention across delays. — replicated within one strong program.
- **Must-read lines sit alone.** Derived: skimming preserves gist but loses details and inferences (Duggan & Payne 2009), and cued attention is selective (Lorch 1989), so critical lines must not sit where scanning is licensed. — derived; no direct experiment located.

## Sentences

- **Plain words.** Oppenheimer 2006, *Applied Cognitive Psychology* 20 (5 experiments): needless complexity lowers judged intelligence of the author, mediated by fluency; Alter & Oppenheimer 2009, *PSPR* (fluency review). Expert audiences: Flammer 2010, *J. Legal Writing Institute* (800 judges: plain version more persuasive 66/34, invariant across seniority); Benson & Kessler 1987, *Loyola LA L. Rev.* (judges rate legalese weaker and infer lower-prestige authors); Trudeau 2012, *Scribes J. Legal Writing* (N=376: ~80% prefer plain; preference rises with education and specialization). — replicated for preference/judgment; comprehension-gain magnitudes come from lay populations (Charrow & Charrow 1979, *Columbia L. Rev.*: rewritten jury instructions +20–30%, losses caused by nominalizations, passives, embeddings).
- **Audience vocabulary / jargon.** Shulman et al. 2020, *J. Language and Social Psychology* (N=650): jargon impedes fluency and engagement, and available definitions do not eliminate the damage. — single, large N; sample is general public. Reconciliation for experts: shared terms are chunks (Cowan 2001), so the rule is audience-relative, not "no technical terms". When ownership is unknown, glossing once is the cheap hedge — derived.
- **One name per thing.** A rotated synonym forces the reader to infer co-reference — the same bridging-inference cost measured by Haviland & Clark 1974; codified as ASD-STE100's one-word-one-meaning rule. — derived + practitioner.
- **Unpack novel noun stacks.** ASD-STE100 caps stacked nouns at three because longer stacks leave the relations between nouns unstated — practitioner; the parsing-load rationale is derived, not directly tested here.
- **One claim per sentence; ~3–4 open threads.** Cowan 2001, *BBS* 24: working memory holds 4±1 chunks; chunk size is knowledge-dependent. Miller's 7±2 was a rhetorical device (Miller 1989 retrospective) and applying either integer to words-per-sentence has no empirical basis. Sentence processing is incremental — the legitimate cap is on simultaneous unresolved dependencies, not words. — replicated (capacity); the prose application is principled extrapolation.
- **Keep the connective.** Sanders & Noordman 2000, *Discourse Processes* 29: explicit relational markers speed processing at no recall cost. Davison & Kantor 1982: deleting connectives to shorten harms comprehension. — replicated.
- **Actors act; unpack nominalizations.** Coleman 1964, *J. Applied Psychology* (transforming nominalizations/passives to active verb forms improved learning); Spyridakis & Isakson 1998, *JTWC* (denominalization aided recall of important content); Charrow & Charrow 1979 (nominalizations among the measured causes of loss). — single each, consistent direction, modest effects; an old but unrefuted literature.
- **Voice: default active, passive licensed by topic.** Ferreira 2003, *Cognitive Psychology* 47: passives misassigned more often under the agent-first heuristic (accuracy still 71–92%). Ferreira 2021, *American Psychologist* 76 ("In Defense of the Passive Voice"): passives serve topic continuity and given-new; blanket prohibition unjustified. Pullum 2014, *Language & Communication* (style-guide anti-passive dogma incoherent; Orwell ~26% passive). — replicated cost, rejected ban.
- **Known first, new last.** Haviland & Clark 1974, *JVLVB* 13: sentences whose given information has a direct antecedent are understood faster; bridging inferences cost time. Clark & Haviland 1977 (the given-new contract). Gopen & Swan 1990, *American Scientist* (topic/stress position) is a practitioner codification of the same mechanism, not itself experimental. — replicated (mechanism), practitioner (the position heuristics).
- **Concrete anchors.** Sadoski, Goetz & Fritz 1993, *JEP* 85 (concreteness → comprehensibility → interest → recall); Sadoski, Goetz & Rodriguez 2000, *JEP* 92 (concreteness the strongest predictor of comprehensibility, interest, recall across four text types); Paivio's dual-coding program. — replicated.
- **Locally complete; cut elaboration, keep cohesion (experts).** McNamara & Kintsch 1996, *Discourse Processes* 22 (reverse cohesion effect: high-knowledge readers learned more from low-cohesion text); O'Reilly & McNamara 2007, *Discourse Processes* 43: the low-cohesion benefit is confined to less-skilled high-knowledge readers — skilled comprehenders benefit from high cohesion. Kalyuga's expertise-reversal effect (2009; 2025 meta-analysis, *Learning and Instruction*): redundant scaffolding hurts experts. — replicated/meta. Rule derived: for expert readers cut spelled-out inferences, never referential/logical links.

## Shape

- **Prose for arguments; lists drain surrounding prose.** Jansen 2014, *Information Design Journal* 21 (5 experiments): list format improved recall of the listed series (benefit shrinks for heterogeneous items) and reduced recall of surrounding text. Morrow, Leirer et al. 1995, *Educational Gerontology*: list-format medication instructions understood and recalled better than paragraph format. Tufte's Columbia/CAIB bullet-point analysis — practitioner case study. — replicated (item benefit), single-program (drain), practitioner (fragmented-argument veto; no experimental counter-evidence found). The consecutive-items clause ("never open with the same words") is NN/g's observed "bypassing" pattern — readers skip lines that open with repeated lead words (Pernice 2019, text-scanning eyetracking). — practitioner.
- **Tables for N×M facts.** Brick, McDowell & Freeman 2020, *Royal Society Open Science* 7:190876 — preregistered RCT, N=2,305 census-matched adults: tables vs identical prose, comprehension d = 0.39, 6-week recall d = 0.12, rated more engaging. Wright 1968 *Ergonomics*, Wright & Fox 1970 (tables beat prose for lookup). — replicated incl. preregistered RCT.
- **Headings and signaling.** Schneider, Beege, Nebel & Rey 2018, *Educational Research Review* 23 — meta, 103 studies, N=12,201: signaling improves retention (g = 0.53) and transfer (g = 0.33); prior knowledge is *not* a moderator (no expertise reversal). Lorch & Lorch 1995/1996, *JEP*: heading benefits appear for complex topic structure, not simple — the threshold in the rule. Sanchez, Lorch & Lorch 2001; Hyönä & Lorch 2004 (eyetracking); Hartley & Trueman 1983 (nine experiments: recall, search, retrieval). — meta + replicated.
- **Bold is a budget that aids finding, not remembering.** NN/g "spotted" scanning pattern (bold attracts fixations) — practitioner. Fowler & Barker 1974, *J. Applied Psychology*: no overall retention gain from highlighting; effects confined to cued content at the expense of uncued. Lorch 1989 review; Dunlosky et al. 2013 rate highlighting low-utility. — replicated null for retention, replicated capture for attention.
- **Short paragraphs at idea boundaries.** Poynter Eyetrack III 2004: 1–2-sentence paragraphs drew over twice the fixations of long-paragraph versions. Frase & Schwartz 1979, *JEP*: segmenting at meaningful boundaries sped verification 14–28% — the active ingredient is the meaningful boundary. — single each, converging.
- **Formatting restraint.** Zhang et al. 2024 (format bias in raters and judges); LMSYS 2024 (markdown coefficients an order of magnitude below length); Wikipedia:Signs of AI writing (bold-term-plus-colon walls, unsolicited headers, Title Case, emoji as tells); OpenAI Model Spec 2025 and Anthropic Claude system prompts (prose-first, minimal formatting, length matched to question) — replicated (bias) + practitioner (deployed cross-lab consensus). Open question: no comprehension RCT of markdown-heavy vs prose chat answers exists yet; the rule rests on bias evidence, tell status, and lab consensus.

## Cut

- **Seductive details.** Rey 2012, *Educational Research Review* 7 — meta, 39 effects: retention d = −0.30, transfer d = −0.48. Sundararajan & Adesope 2020, *Educational Psychology Review* — meta, 68 effects: g ≈ −0.33. Confirmed again by a 2025 multi-level meta in *EPR*. — meta; the strongest single finding in this file.
- **Performative sentences and puffery.** Wikipedia:Signs of AI writing (significance inflation: "stands as a testament", "plays a vital role") — practitioner; Kobak et al. 2025 excess-vocabulary corpus (below) supplies the measured lexicon. — practitioner + replicated corpus.
- **Hedges must carry information.** Kim, Liao, Vorvoreanu, Ballard & Wortman Vaughan, FAccT 2024 (preregistered, N=404): first-person specific hedges ("I'm not sure, but…") reduced overreliance and increased user accuracy; generic hedges weaker or nonsignificant — wording matters. npj Digital Medicine 2025: boilerplate disclaimers collapsed from 26.3% (2022) to 0.97% (2025) of outputs — the industry abandoned them. — single (Kim) + corpus.
- **Padding correlates with wrongness.** Zhang, Das & Zhang 2024, "Verbosity ≠ Veracity" (arXiv 2411.07858): verbosity compensation in all models tested (GPT-4: 50.4% of responses); verbose answers show higher uncertainty and up to 27.6pp lower accuracy. — single/preprint.
- **Simplification measurably helps comprehension.** Guidroz et al. 2025 (Google), arXiv 2505.01980 — randomized, N=4,563, 31 texts: simplified versions raised comprehension accuracy (+3.9pp overall, +14.6pp on PubMed texts) and lowered cognitive load. — single, large.

## Register

- **The trust penalty is real and persistent.** Altay & Gilardi 2024, *PNAS Nexus* (2 preregistered experiments, N≈5,000): AI-labeled headlines rated less accurate and shared less regardless of truth or actual origin. "The AI Disclosure Penalty" 2025/26 (16 preregistered experiments, N=27,491, within-paper meta): devaluation attenuates but never disappears. "The Transparency Dilemma", *OBHDP* 2025 (13 experiments): disclosure erodes trust. — meta/replicated.
- **Readers judge by tells, not content.** Jakesch, Hancock & Naaman 2023, *PNAS* (N=4,600): detection 50–52%; judgments driven by flawed surface heuristics. Jones & Bergen 2025 (arXiv 2503.23674): persona-prompted GPT-4.5 judged human 73% of the time; the same model without the persona ~36%, baseline GPT-4o 21% — register, not capability, is what reads as AI. — replicated. This is why register rules are load-bearing, not cosmetic.
- **Fingerprint lexicon.** Kobak et al. 2025, *Science Advances* (10.1126/sciadv.adt3813): >15M PubMed abstracts; abrupt post-LLM excess of style words ("delve", "underscore", "showcase", "intricate", "meticulous", "boast"…); ≥13.5% of 2024 abstracts LLM-processed. Juzek & Ward, COLING 2025 (arXiv 2412.11385): 21 focal words traced to post-training. — replicated corpus.
- **Construction catalog.** Wikipedia:Signs of AI writing + WikiProject AI Cleanup (field-tested since 2023; editors caution single signs aren't proof): negative parallelism, rule-of-three, copula avoidance ("serves as"), trailing-participle analysis, essay-conjunction chains, summary closers, formula sections, vague authority, question-restating openers. — practitioner, corroborated by the corpus studies.
- **Sycophancy costs trust.** Carro 2024 (arXiv 2412.02802): sycophantic variant lowered reported and behavioral trust. Sun et al., CHI 2026 (arXiv 2502.10844, N=224): complimentary demeanor + stance-adapting reduced authenticity and trust. "The Rise of Verbal Tics" 2026 (arXiv 2604.19139, 160k responses, 120 raters): sycophancy–naturalness r = −0.87. OpenAI's April 2025 sycophancy rollback — natural experiment. — replicated (+preprint).
- **Fluency suppresses scrutiny.** Kabir, Udo-Imeh, Kou & Zhang, CHI 2024 (arXiv 2308.02312): 52% of 517 ChatGPT answers to Stack Overflow questions contained wrong information; users overlooked it 39% of the time and credited the "well-articulated", comprehensive style. — single, landmark. Basis of "checkable, not impressive".
- **Em-dash density.** Freeburg 2026 (arXiv 2603.27006, preprint): em dashes persist even when models suppress other formatting — a fine-tuning signature (0–9.1 per 1k words across 12 models); OpenAI shipped a user-facing fix Nov 2025 after the tell became public shorthand. — preprint + practitioner. The rule targets density, not the character.

## Debunked — do not re-admit

- **Sentence-length comprehension percentages** ("90%+ at 14 words, <10% at 43" — American Press Institute): no locatable primary source, author, date, or method; circulates only in secondhand marketing citations; GOV.UK's own research-background page cites no study for its 25-word figure. Direction (shorter easier) fine; the numbers are folklore.
- **7±2 words/items per sentence**: Miller's 7 was rhetorical (Miller 1989); capacity is ~4 chunks (Cowan 2001); no direct application to prose exists.
- **Desirable-difficulty fonts / added friction for depth**: Diemand-Yauman 2011 failed replication at scale (Meyer et al. 2015, 13 labs, N=7,365; Rummer et al. 2016); Sans Forgetica nulls (Taylor 2020, Wetzler 2021, Geller 2022). Ease of processing is not the enemy of depth.
- **Never use the passive**: Pullum 2014; Ferreira 2021.
- **Jargon is fine if you define it**: Shulman et al. 2020 — definitions did not restore fluency or engagement.
- **Write to a readability-formula score**: Davison & Kantor 1982; Redish & Schriver — formulas measure correlates, not causes; editing to the score can harm comprehension.
- **Design for the F-pattern**: inverts NN/g's finding — the F-pattern is what readers fall back on when formatting fails, and it causes missed content.
- **Experts want dense, formal, jargon-rich prose**: Trudeau 2012 (plain-language preference rises with expertise); Loranger/NN/g 2017; Schneider 2018 (no expertise reversal for signaling); Flammer 2010.
- **Hook readers with vivid asides**: the seductive-details meta-analyses.
- **Highlighting improves retention**: Fowler & Barker 1974; Dunlosky et al. 2013 — emphasis aids locating, not remembering.
- **"People read 25% slower on screens"**: CRT-era; the modern, supported effect is a small comprehension deficit for expository text (Delgado 2018), not a speed constant.
- **"Users preferred it" as a style arbiter**: length, format, and sycophancy biases in human preference data (Singhal 2023; Zhang 2024; Sharma 2024) make raw preference wins unusable as evidence of reader value.

## Generalization caveats

- Comprehension effect sizes mostly come from students and general-public samples reading instructional text. For expert readers the directly measured benefits of plain style are preference, persuasion, speed, and credibility; comprehension gains are inferred. No finding located runs the other way.
- Chat and terminal reading is under-measured: NN/g's AI-chat studies are qualitative (N=8–9); no large-N eyetracking of chat answers exists; "words until abandonment" is unstudied. Terminal rendering can't carry heading hierarchies or reliable bold — ordering, line breaks, and blank-line segmentation (Frase & Schwartz, the most medium-independent result) do the structural work there.
- The pro-list, pro-table, pro-pyramid evidence is about lookup, procedures, and fact extraction; causal explanation and teaching novices favor connected prose and sometimes narrative order (Emde 2016; Jansen's drain; Lorch's nulls for simple structure).
- Several load-bearing 2026 sources (YapBench, The Last Fingerprint, Verbal Tics) are unreviewed preprints; their grades say so.
- A "0.57/0.53 human markdown preference" figure sometimes attributed to The Last Fingerprint does not appear in that paper. Do not cite it.
